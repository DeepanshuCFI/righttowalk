# Right to Walk · Footpath Check (Phase 0 + Phase 1)

Crashfree India's citizen "right to walk" street check. One stretch of road, eleven plain questions, the stretch drawn live as you answer, a result in the Supreme Court's own audit vocabulary, and a notice to the owning agency.

Design direction "kerb and chalk": the street's own materials (concrete pavers, black-and-yellow kerb paint, tactile yellow, chalk marks, the pedestrian signal). Khand + Mukta, both Latin and Devanagari. Deliberately not the Crashfree India design system (Deep, 23 Oct 2026: citizen and crowdsourcing products match their audience).

## Interaction
Two modes, chosen first: **Quick check** (2 min: location, which footpath, tap what is at the spot, one photo, one yes/no; produces a spot report and a short notice) and **Full stretch** (15 min: location, which footpath, four road-type taps, land use; the eleven items; "End here" marks the end of the stretch and computes its length; score, strip, full notice). Location is taken with `navigator.geolocation` (rounded to 4 decimals, about 10 m; nothing is recorded until the button is tapped). Inside the claude.ai artifact viewer the location prompt is refused (no location capability exists for this account), so the page falls back to pasting coordinates from Google Maps or naming the road; on a deployed site it is the normal browser prompt. Start and end fixes give the stretch length and a compass heading, which the notice prints with an OpenStreetMap link. Clause numbers sit behind a "Why it matters" tap on each item, and weights behind "How is this scored?" on the result.
Item 2 is a tap palette: each tap adds one problem to `st.obs` and drops its glyph onto the live strip; a minus removes one. Items 2 and 7 get a suggested answer from what was tapped (flagged `st.auto`, shown as a note) which the user can override. The result and the notice list the recorded problems with counts and the agencies concerned, and compose the item 2 and 7 asks from the types present. Motion: drop-in on new glyphs, striding walker while answers land, reveal wipe on strips, lamps and the signal animate on, score counts up, chalk marks write on; all guarded by prefers-reduced-motion.

## Method v1.0 (record tag v1.2 once obstruction types are included)
Weights: footpath 20, clear 15, crossing 15, speed 12, width 8, ramps 8, surface 7, lighting 7, kerb and continuity 4, buffer 4. Score scaled over judgeable items; "partly" is half weight on clear, crossing and surface; lighting may be "can't tell". Gate = 100 m without stepping onto the road; a failed gate is "not walkable" regardless of score. If there is no footpath, the footpath-dependent items fail automatically. Answer tiles carry no red or green; semantic colour appears only on the result.

Legal text is quoted verbatim from Maniyar Iliyaz v. P. Ayyappan (2026 INSC 647) and the Rajaseekaran orders of 14 May 2025 and 7 Oct 2025. When a full check fails on ramps and tactile tiles (item 6), the notice also cites section 41(1)(c) of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (text verified on Indian Kanoon, 24 Oct 2026), the Harmonised Guidelines and Standards for Universal Accessibility in India, 2021 under rule 15 of the RPwD Rules, 2017, and IRC:SP:117-2018, Manual on Universal Accessibility for Urban Roads and Streets. IRC:103-2022 numbers come from the NITI-hosted IRC draft; the 2012 edition was read in full. Research: `~/Documents/Claude/reports/Right to walk auditor India.md`.

## The legal ladder
The learn page carries "Who can make the law, and who has": eight rungs from the constitutional right (Articles 19(1)(d) and 21, Maniyar; Article 21, Rajaseekaran 14 May 2025) through legislative competence (Seventh Schedule, List II, entry 13), the municipal duty (Article 243W, Twelfth Schedule item 4; the HMC Act 1994, GMDA Act 2017, HSVP, NHAI), the unused State powers in the Motor Vehicles Act (section 138(1A): rules for pedestrians' access to public places; section 210D: design, construction and maintenance standards for roads other than national highways; both inserted 2019, six-month direction of 7 Oct 2025 lapsed), liability under section 198A (fine up to one lakh rupees where non-compliance with prescribed standards causes death or disability; after the Jan Vishwas amendment of 2026 the section names standards under section 210C for highways and section 210D by the State), accessibility (RPwD Act s.41(1)(c), Harmonised Guidelines 2021, IRC:SP:117-2018), standards (IRC:103-2022, binding only where adopted) and the Court's directions. MV Act texts verified against the `mv-act-counsel` corpus (India Code consolidation as on 21 May 2025 plus the JV-2026 amended sections). The "no Right to Walk Act" line now says where the power and the duty sit instead. Hindi in `src/learn.js`.

## Deaths on record
`src/deaths.json` (published as `data/deaths.json`, inlined into the learn page, the map and the report as `DEATHS`) lists pedestrians killed in Gurugram at places where a public record states what the road lacked. Rules of entry, also printed on the report page: a date and a place to about 100 m; age band and activity as reported, a minor's name only where the family has spoken publicly, never a photograph; the infrastructure fact quoted from the record, never inferred; two independent reports or one plus an official record; other factors recorded as reported, no blame added; live cases marked. `src/deaths.js` matches each case to published checks within 60 m (walked, failing). The map draws them as black marks with a chalk ring (`#d=<id>` deep-links one); the report carries the line under the headline and the table; "walk it" opens `index.html?at=lat,lon&road=…#check`, which pre-fills the spot.
