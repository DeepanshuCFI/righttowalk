What this number is, and is not

  • It counts eligible full checks, not kilometres or unique segments. Quick reports about one spot are excluded. Two checks of the same segment count twice. A missing end location means distance was not measured.
  • The sample chose itself. People choose where to check. Even if all 100 listed roads receive one check, that does not make the sample representative of Gurugram or establish coverage of either footpath side.
  • Moderator review is not a site measurement. A linked second field visit supports a verified label; older labels may need their source record checked. Every public result remains a dated observation.
  • The Court states the duty; this project defines the distance. Maniyar Iliyaz, paragraph 20(b) calls for demarcated, well-maintained footpaths. The 100 m continuity check and score are Right to Walk's own method.

    What we are asking for

    • Publish a measured Gurugram audit. Start with named segments and high-footfall or recent pedestrian-injury locations, record both sides and remedies, and publish the underlying measurements. Any pilot-town request should cite the signed order and current docket once obtained.
    • Audit the city's footpaths on the Court's terms, and publish it. On 7 October 2025 (S. Rajaseekaran, 2025 INSC 1189) the Court ordered road-owning agencies in India's fifty largest cities, and NHAI, to audit footpaths for width, height, surface and encroachment hotspots. Gurugram is not one of the fifty, so GMDA, MCG and HSVP are not yet under that order; NHAI's stretches here are. We ask the three agencies to run the same audit, road by road, and publish it, so that citizens' checks and the agencies' own can be laid side by side.
    • State which standard and agency apply to each road. Publish road ownership and the applicable adopted or binding design provisions; verify figures against the final published IRC edition before using them as compliance findings.

    Method and data

    Footpath Check method v1.0; record tags v1.2 and v1.3 may coexist. Quick mode records whether one spot can be passed. Full mode asks about 100 m continuity and ten weighted items (footpath 20, clear 15, crossing 15, speed 12, width 8, ramps 8, surface 7, lighting 7, kerb and continuity 4, buffer 4). Its score is an exploratory project index, not a legal compliance or crash-risk measure. Straight-line start-to-end distance is shown only when both fixes exist; it is not distance walked. Locations are rounded to four decimals (about 10 m); moderators read published rows; names are not published.
