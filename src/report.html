What this number is, and is not

  • It counts stretches, not kilometres. Each check is one side of one stretch of road, walked by one person on one day. Two checks of the same stretch count twice.
  • The sample chose itself. People check the footpaths they walk and the ones that annoy them. Until the 100 roads in Haryana's own audit list have each been checked, the share above describes the record, not the city.
  • "Verified" means re-walked. A trained volunteer re-walks a share of the record; the verified share is shown above. Where the re-walk disagrees, the verified result stands.
  • The test is the Court's. "If the road exists, there is a duty to ensure that there are demarcated and well-maintained footpaths for walkers" (Maniyar Iliyaz v. P. Ayyappan, 2026 INSC 647, para 20(b)). A footpath you cannot stay on for 100 m is not that.

Haryana's own list

The sampling frame. On 8 October 2026 the state gave GMDA, MCG and HSVP thirty days to upgrade 100 Gurugram roads of at least 500 m each, footpaths included, with a QR-coded board on each naming the officer responsible; the deadline is 7 November. Only GMDA's 27 stretches have been published. A check counts for a road when it starts within 60 m of it. Assignment sheet · on the map.

What we are asking for

  • Name Gurugram as one of the five pilot towns the Supreme Court asked the Union to identify on 5 October 2026 in Re: Fundamental Right to Walk and Footpath, W.P.(C) 844/2026. This record is the baseline a pilot needs.
  • Audit the city's footpaths on the Court's terms, and publish it. On 7 October 2025 (S. Rajaseekaran, 2025 INSC 1189) the Court ordered road-owning agencies in India's fifty largest cities, and NHAI, to audit footpaths for width, height, surface and encroachment hotspots. Gurugram is not one of the fifty, so GMDA, MCG and HSVP are not yet under that order; NHAI's stretches here are. We ask the three agencies to run the same audit, road by road, and publish it, so that citizens' checks and the agencies' own can be laid side by side.
  • Adopt one footpath standard for the city (IRC:103-2022, the code the Court named) and say which agency owns which road, so that a notice from a resident reaches the right desk the first time.

Method and data

Footpath Check method v1.0 (record tag v1.2). Gate: 100 m on the footpath without stepping onto the road. Ten weighted items for a full check (footpath 20, clear 15, crossing 15, speed 12, width 8, ramps 8, surface 7, lighting 7, kerb and continuity 4, buffer 4); a quick check records one spot, the problems on it and the gate. Locations are rounded to four decimals (about 10 m). Every published row was read by a moderator; names are never published.
