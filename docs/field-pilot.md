# Field pilot for the Footpath Check

Status: proposed protocol, 30 October 2026. No field study has been run under this document. The pilot tests whether the questions produce consistent, safe, inspectable observations. It does not estimate the share of Gurugram roads that fail.

## Sampling and people

1. Before any visits, list 12–20 named 100 m segments with fixed start/end landmarks, road side, road class, land use, likely owner and a segment ID. Spread them across agencies and neighbourhoods; include high-footfall places and locations identified in official pedestrian injury or fatality records where sources permit. Do not choose only visibly damaged stretches.
2. Audit both sides separately. Assign a second independent auditor to at least one third of segments; the two should not see each other's answers before submitting.
3. Review the instrument and route plan with wheelchair users, blind or low-vision walkers, older walkers, women who walk after dark, and caregivers. Do not ask anyone to traverse a segment they judge unsafe or inaccessible. Record that as an observation from a safe position.
4. Distinguish vendors from blocked clear walking space. A person or lawful livelihood is not the audit unit; the route condition is.

## Visit protocol

- Brief auditors on the difference between a **spot report**, the project's **100 m continuous-walk question**, and a measured route. Demonstrate "not observed" and "cannot tell" answers. Do not infer traffic speed, width, ownership or lighting from a daytime photograph.
- Record date and time, segment ID and side, start/end landmark descriptions, GPS fixes with accuracy if available, and whether the auditor actually traversed the 100 m. A straight-line GPS separation is not walked distance.
- Stop in a safe place to use the phone or take photos. Never ask an auditor to use the form while crossing or standing in traffic. Record where the route becomes inaccessible without forcing a traverse.
- Record the ten scored items and the separate 100 m answer. For a failure, identify the first point where a walker had to enter the road or stop, and one photo where safe. Photos are optional and must avoid identifiable bystanders where possible.
- Assign a second observation a new record ID linked to the first. Retain both item sets, visit dates, auditor roles, and a reason for any adjudicated change; do not silently replace a citizen record with a verification flag.

## Analysis and revision

Report counts by item and mode, missing/"cannot tell" answers, completion and abandonment times, GPS accuracy, photo adequacy, safety interruptions, and disagreements on paired visits. Show raw agreement counts as well as a suitable inter-rater statistic if sample size permits. Review disagreements with participants before changing wording. Pre-register a practical revision rule: any core item with repeated interpretation disagreements or a safety-related abandonment gets rewritten and tested again before a public failure percentage uses it.

Present pilot results by named segment and side, with dates and coverage. Keep unvisited portions unobserved. A convenience or assigned pilot is evidence about those places and the instrument, not a representative estimate for Gurugram. Cite the published method version, source ledger, and limitations beside any exported dossier.
